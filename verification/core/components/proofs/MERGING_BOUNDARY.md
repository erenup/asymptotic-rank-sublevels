# Exact boundary under a four-to-three-mode grouping

For every a with 3<=a<4, the asymptotic-rank sublevel in C^9 tensor C^3 tensor C^2 is exactly the determinantal variety where the C^9 | (C^3 tensor C^2) flattening has rank at most three. In particular it is irreducible.

Proof. Every matrix flattening rank is a lower bound on asymptotic tensor rank, so every tensor in the sublevel has that flattening rank at most three. Conversely, compress its nine-dimensional factor to the at-most-three-dimensional image support, padding back to dimension three if necessary. This preserves asymptotic rank by the local inclusion/retraction argument. Every tensor in C^3 tensor C^3 tensor C^2 has border rank at most three: view it as a pair (A,B) of 3-by-3 matrices. The nonempty Zariski open where A is invertible and A^(-1)B has distinct eigenvalues consists of simultaneously equivalent diagonal pairs (I,diag(lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3)). Each has rank at most three by its displayed three diagonal summands. This open is dense in the affine space of all pairs; since the border-rank-at-most-three locus is closed and contains it, it is the entire space. Asymptotic rank is at most border rank, so the compressed tensor has asymptotic rank at most three, hence at most a. Finally, the rank-at-most-three 9-by-6 determinantal variety is irreducible: it is the image of pairs of matrices (U,V) with U of size 9-by-3 and V of size 3-by-6 under (U,V)->UV, and its source is irreducible. Every rank-at-most-three matrix has such a factorization. QED.

Grouping any two of the three three-dimensional modes in format (3,3,3,2) gives (9,3,2). For a_0=62^(1/3)<=a<4, the original four-mode sublevel is reducible by the established witness, whereas the full grouped three-mode sublevel is irreducible by the proposition. This is an explicit ambient boundary, stronger than merely warning that grouping can lose rank. It does not assert that grouping maps the original sublevel onto the full grouped sublevel.
